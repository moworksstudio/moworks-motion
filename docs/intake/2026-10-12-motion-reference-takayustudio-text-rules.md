# Intake: text rules for the skill (from the TakayuStudio text guides)

Part of the intake [2026-10-12-motion-reference-takayustudio.md](2026-10-12-motion-reference-takayustudio.md). Rules in our own words, proposed
for the desktop session to weigh; nothing here changes the skill until the user agrees. Sources: the site's `data/text/guides.js` and
`data/text/research.js` as served on 2026-10-12 (read as text), which in turn cite Netflix, BBC, Adobe, MDN, Remotion and others.

Read: `takayu/site/data/text/guides.js` (12 guides: splitting, timing-readability, easing-for-text, accessibility, japanese, css-only,
js-libraries, after-effects, remotion-video, canvas-webgl-svg, checklist, glossary; English bodies) and `research.js` (notes `web` and
`motion`). Checked against `workflow/film.md`, `workflow/recipes.md`, `workflow/story.md`, `workflow/review.md`, `reference/*.md`,
`library/README.md`, `SKILL.md`, and the code where a rule touches it (`engine/runtime/spec-runtime.js`, `engine/qa/buildlint.py`,
`engine/vendor/libs/aimk/eases.js`, `library/presets/text/captions`, `library/presets/lower/news-ticker`, `engine/kit.py` export table).

Left out: web-only material (screen readers, reduced motion, pause controls, scroll-driven CSS, choosing a JS library), Japanese-only
typesetting (vertical writing, ruby, kinsoku, tate-chu-yoko), and points the kit already states (ease-out entrances, exits shorter and
eased in, blur at most 10 px on text and on the wrapper, 4.5:1 contrast, 3 flashes a second, seeded randomness, fonts loaded before a
frame, Vietnamese diacritics room in masks).

## 2. Facts to check before use

1. **Caption reading rate.** The kit uses 17 cps; the current Netflix English (USA) guide says 20 for adults, 17 for children; older
   material says 17 / 13. No source gives Vietnamese numbers: read Netflix's Vietnamese Timed Text Style Guide (cps, characters per line)
   before setting limits for Vietnamese captions.
2. **Minimum subtitle duration differs by source:** 5/6 s (Netflix general), 0.5 s (Netflix Japanese), 0.7 s (kit).
3. **The 2-frame gap** is stated at 24 fps. Whether it stays 2 frames or becomes about 83 ms (5 frames) at 60 fps is not said; the
   research table converts the 0.5 s threshold by time (12 f at 24 = 30 f at 60). Check the Netflix timing page.
4. **Marked as unverified convention in the research note** ("要検証"): 20–60 ms a letter and 80–150 ms a line; punctuation pauses
   (+100–200 / +250–400 ms); the telop pop 0 → 110 → 100 % in 4–6 frames (frame rate not given: at 30 fps that is 8–12 frames, 0.13–0.2 s, at
   60); short-form captions of 1–3 words with 100–200 ms pops; outline 8–15 % of the font size; the ~1 Hz cursor (only the cursor
   expression itself was confirmed); 50–150 ms between strokes (a kanji convention; the Vietnamese mark order in rule 24 is our own
   carry-over).
5. **Derived, not standard:** the "read twice" hold (0.6 s a word, minimum 1.5 s) is the source's own working formula from an SSW rule and
   Netflix numbers; the overshoot budget (10 % scale, 0.1–0.2 em, 5–10°) is its rule of thumb from implementations. The lower third's
   5–7 s and 2 s glance come from one blog post.
6. **Our spring arithmetic** (rule 6) is the textbook step response of a spring released at rest. A spring that starts with velocity, or
   the kit's time-scaled spring eases in `eases.js`, may differ: graph `springk(220, 18)` with `kit.py measure --graph` before writing
   it in. Checked by calculation: GSAP `back.out(1.70158)` overshoots 10.0 %; the kit's cubic back.out (0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1) 9.8 %;
   `text/captions` back.out(2.2) 15.4 % of a 0.7 → 1 travel (peak scale about 1.046: inside the budget).
7. **Whole px per frame** comes from a credit-roll article for editing systems that snap to pixels. Chromium draws fractional
   translations sub-pixel, so in kit renders the artefact may be shimmer or softness rather than judder. Test a ticker at 6.56 vs 6 px a
   frame in MP4 (no blur) and MOV (180° blur) before making rule 16 binding.
8. **Text-stroke width "does not interpolate"** (japanese guide) holds for CSS animations; the kit's GSAP tweens write the inline value
   every frame, so it probably does animate in the kit. Test it; it was left out of the table.
9. **Reading-speed sources:** the BBC numbers (160–180 wpm, 68 % line width) come from a third-party summary (bbc.co.uk was blocked for
   the author); RSVP rates in the literature range from 100 to 1500 wpm.
10. **Localisation length:** the only expansion figure given is German about 30 % longer than English; the Vietnamese ↔ English ratio is
    not in the sources: measure it on the studio's own bilingual copy.
11. **Phrase-break research** (F-score 74 % vs 21 %) is a Japanese live-subtitle study; that it carries over to Vietnamese is assumed.
12. **Recogniser lag for speech:** the kit measured 80 ms on sung attacks; the guide gives no number for speech ("a few tens of ms").
    Measure on a voice-over before adding a lead to `transcribe`.
